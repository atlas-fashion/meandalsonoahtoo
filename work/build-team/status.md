# Build record — circular Wilson maze

Lead: current session, Opus 5.5. Started 2026-09-25.

## Goal and scope

Replace the level's maze with a procedural **circular** maze generated by
Wilson's algorithm (loop-erased random walk), integrated into the running Godot
4.7.2 game — not pseudocode, not a demo in isolation.

Engine facts (confirmed, not assumed): Godot 4.7.2, GDScript, Forward+/Vulkan.
`scripts/maze.gd` (~1750 lines) builds the level and owns the tile grid that the
spawner, jail cells, gates, chests and navmesh all key off. `scripts/enemy.gd`
drives AI through `NavigationAgent3D` over a baked navmesh, with CHASE / WINDUP /
ATTACK / RECOVER / STAGGER / SUMMON states, wander areas and dormancy.

Target hardware: Quadro P620, 3440×1440. The project's `CLAUDE.md` is binding —
in particular the game is **per-pixel bound, not geometry bound**, so wall
geometry must not be "optimised" by cutting draw distance.

### Deliverables

1. Generator + project integration.
2. Playable demonstration using existing controls.
3. Configuration with sensible defaults.
4. Shared AI navigation interface, **alongside** the existing navmesh AI.
5. Debug visualisation and focused validation.
6. Short explanation: regenerate a seed, adjust difficulty, connect another AI mode.

### Acceptance criteria

- Wilson's with loop erasure, not DFS/Prim's, and no bias toward centre or exit.
- Graph genuinely circular; adjacency only across shared arcs of positive length.
- Tree by default: N cells ⇒ N−1 passages, connected, acyclic.
- Same config + seed ⇒ identical maze.
- Clearance validated; impossible configs refused with a reason.
- Existing AI, spawner, cells and gates keep working.

## Direction baseline (approved)

From the user's spec, verbatim where it constrains design:

- "Do not generate a rectangular maze and visually bend it into a circle."
- "Do not substitute randomized depth-first search, Prim's algorithm, or another generator."
- "Do not bias random-walk steps toward the center or exit."
- "Do not invent or replace my existing AI modes. Expose the information they need."
- "Corner contact alone must not create adjacency."
- "Prioritize correct topology, navigable geometry, reproducibility, and responsive generation before visual polish."

### What the gates caught that the lead had shipped

would have reached the game:

### 2026-09-28 — invisible wall, per-instance light, low-poly walls, phases

**The invisible wall was systematic, not occasional, and my first fix missed
it.** Every collision box was CENTRED on the wall contour with 1.6 m of
thickness, so it reached **0.8 m into the corridor along every wall in the
level**. A chest-room doorway lost 1.6 m of width to the two sides closing in.

It survived the previous round because the test only sampled corridor
CENTRELINES, which sit 2.2 m from the boundary and never entered the intruding
shell. The replacement sweeps the whole walkable region on a 0.5 m grid.
Measured, fixing one cause at a time:

| change | blocked of 38,329 standable points | deepest intrusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| as shipped | 1,964 | 1.04 m |
| box pushed behind the wall face | 977 | 1.73 m |
| along-run padding split from thickness | 206 | 1.52 m |
| adaptive subdivision at hard bends | 163 | 1.52 m |
| thickness clamped to available rock | 24 | 0.65 m |
| rock probed along the whole arc | **14** | **0.60 m** |

The last two mattered most and were not bends at all: where the rock between
two corridors is thinner than the box, pushing it outward punched it through
into the passage beyond. Negative control confirms the sweep still catches the
original fault (1,964 / 1.04 m → FAIL).

**Per-instance baked lighting** (`scripts/organic_lighting.gd`) instead of a
lightmap, after the frozen-level route proved unusable: 497 MB of video memory
against 251 MB for the whole game, and simply having the 382 MB file in the
project took the editor down three times with `Vulkan device was lost`. Each
panel gets one baked colour from the torches that reach it, written as
MultiMesh instance colour; the 209 lights then skip static geometry via
`light_cull_mask` and keep lighting only dynamic objects.

Controlled A/B on identical geometry:

| | bake off | bake on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| spawn corridor | 15.18 ms | **13.20 ms** |
| boss room edge | 16.46 ms | **15.61 ms** |
| overall | 64.3 fps | **67.9 fps** |

Real, but about 2 ms rather than the ~5 ms estimated. No shadows, no bounce.

**Walls remade at 180 triangles**, down from 320 and from 1,199 before that,
and thinned 0.857 → 0.5 m so neighbouring panels step less against each other.

**Phase seam** (`scripts/maze_phases.gd`) for boss-gated expansion. A duck-typed
`View` answers `inside()`/`distance()` as though locked corridors were never
generated, so the contour, collision, grid and navmesh all follow the open
region with no downstream changes. Verified at 3 phases: 39 / 102 / 115

always in the last phase — so restricting the map stranded the player in locked
rock at 0.0% reachability. The spawn now moves to the deepest open node.

### 2026-09-27 — brick kit, layout, playability, baked lighting

**Both reported faults were one bug.** "An invisible wall entering a chest
room" and "a dead end into a wall with no brick texture" both came from the
contour raster being sized exactly to `organic_extent` while the generator
places geometry past it — dead-end spurs reach 22 m from their parent. Out
there the boundary could not close and collision was thrown across the
corridor. Measured: **43 of 7,268 centreline points sealed, every one at the
map edge, none at a chamber rim**. A 16 m raster margin took it to **0**.
Separately ruled out: panel coverage (0.00 m gap) and panel facing (0 of 6,990
reversed), so neither caused the untextured wall.

**Kit regenerated** — large dark grey brick, broken brick, broken ceiling,
broken floor. Two generator misses corrected by cropping harder: the bricks
came back too small (cropping leaves fewer, larger blocks) and the ceiling came
back as a tray with a raised rim *again*. The pale barred-window variant was
retired; it clashed with dark grey.

**Layout**, measured over the corridor graph with Dijkstra:

| | before | after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| chest rooms | 10 | 6 |
| corridor distance to nearest other chest room, min | 29 m | **75 m** |
| mean | 74 m | **110 m** |

Long links cut 3 → 1: each is a shortcut that pulls two chest rooms together,
which is the opposite of the ask.

**Performance: 71.5 fps, worst p99 18.06 ms, 251 MB.** 0 errors, 0 warnings,
grid 100% reachable.

**Baked lighting — built, not baked.** Two hard constraints, both established
by probing rather than assumption:

- `LightmapGI` exposes only `set_light_data` / `get_light_data` /
  `bake_quality` to scripting. **`bake()` is editor-only**, so the final step
  cannot be automated.
- `LightmapGI` cannot light a `MultiMeshInstance3D`, and a lightmap needs fixed
  geometry — so the level must be frozen to one seed and flattened into unique
  static meshes.

`tools/freeze_level.gd` does everything up to the bake: builds seed 20260918,
merges all 275 chunks into static meshes with instance transforms baked in,
generates UV2 with `ArrayMesh.lightmap_unwrap()` (**0 failures over 2,227,694
triangles**), switches all 209 torches to `BAKE_STATIC`, adds a `LightmapGI`,
and saves `scenes/level_baked.scn`.

**The measured cost: 497 MB of video memory against 251 MB for the whole
instanced game**, and a 382 MB scene file. It loads in 0.3 s and fits in 2 GB,
but it roughly triples the geometry budget. What it buys back is 209 omni
lights that stop being evaluated per frame — worth an estimated ~5 ms, given
that cutting 347 torches to 133 previously saved 3.7 ms.

Saving as `.tscn` first wrote **510 MB of ASCII vertex data**; binary `.scn` is
required. The frozen scene stamps `freeze_seed` in its metadata and `maze.gd`
errors loudly if it does not match `maze_seed`, because a mismatch desyncs
geometry from the grid silently.

### Benchmark — kit only, Quadro P620, 3440×1440

Standing at the player spawn, looking down the corridor. No torches, enemies,
physics, glow or SSAO, so this is a **floor** for the finished level, not a
prediction of it.

```
everything          avg 15.22 ms   median 15.29 ms   p99 15.67 ms   223 calls  182,800 prims
walls off           avg  8.46 ms   median  8.45 ms   p99  9.19 ms
ceiling off         avg 12.64 ms   median 12.78 ms   p99 13.15 ms
nothing drawn       avg  1.79 ms   median  1.74 ms   p99  2.47 ms
render scale 0.5    avg 13.20 ms   median 13.32 ms   p99 13.70 ms

  walls off          -6.79 ms
  ceiling off        -2.61 ms
  render scale 0.5   -2.04 ms      fixed per-frame cost 1.79 ms
```

**An earlier reading of 27.26 ms for the same scene was wrong** and has been
replaced. It was taken while the Godot editor was reimporting the kit in the
background and competing for the GPU. Three consecutive clean runs now return
15.22 / 15.22 / 15.22 ms, so the figures above are stable to two decimals. The
original number was reported to the user and has been corrected.

**This is new data that changes a settled finding.** `CLAUDE.md` records that
the game is per-pixel bound and that render scale beats everything else
(−6.87 ms at 0.5 against an 11.42 ms baseline). That held for the old box-built
level. It does **not** hold for the kit: halving the render scale here saves
only 2.04 ms of 15.22, while the content itself accounts for 13.4 ms at 182,800
primitives across 223 draw calls. **The kit level is geometry-bound, not
fill-bound**, so optimisation has to come from instance count, LOD and
occlusion — not from resolution. `CLAUDE.md` should be amended once the level
ships and the figure is confirmed in the real game rather than in the preview.

Two process notes, both of which `CLAUDE.md` already warned about:

- **The first ablation was vsync.** "Walls off" and "render scale 0.5" returned
  identical numbers — 16.70 / 16.72 ms median, 33.55 / 33.60 ms p99, one and two
  60 Hz refresh intervals. Setting `VSYNC_DISABLED` once in `_initialize()` did
  not stick; it is now re-asserted and read back at every station, and the
  script errors if the mode is not actually off.
- **Chunking the MultiMeshes bought nothing.** The first run reported 7 draw
  calls and 7,400 primitives, which looked like a map-spanning AABB defeating
  the culler. Splitting into 24 m chunks made the counters honest (223 calls,
  264,900 primitives) and moved the frame time from 27.16 to 27.24 ms — noise.
  Kept, because the counters are now meaningful and it is what per-chunk
  occluders will need, but it is **not** a speed-up and is not claimed as one.

### What the numeric checks passed and the renders caught

Three separate defects cleared every automated check and were only visible in a
render. This is the `CLAUDE.md` rule about not verifying by reading the diff,
and it earned its place again here:

- **The wall panel was a cloud of loose shards.** Exact size, exact triangle
  count, no warning. Tripo's mesh has coincident unwelded vertices, so collapse
  decimation tore it instead of simplifying it. Fixed by welding first.
- **The ceiling was an open-topped tray, not a slab.** Regenerated with negative
  constraints in the prompt.
- **The barred window had no bars.** The grate survives at 2,000 triangles and
  is gone at 900, so that piece carries its own budget.

A fourth was caught by measuring the *file* rather than the scene: the exporter
discards degenerate triangles, so the bounding box prep scaled from was inflated
by junk and the panel shipped 11% short. `prep_kit_asset.py` now re-imports what
it wrote and fails if it is more than 2 mm off target. Confirmed non-vacuous:
with the cleanup disabled it fails at 0.0938 m and exits 1.

## Art direction pass

Asked for: more rustic stone, softer firelight, darker and more atmospheric.

- Albedo maps are desaturated only PARTLY (0.70, not 0.88) and pushed for
  contrast (1.35). Full desaturation gave flat poured-concrete stone; the
  leftover colour variation and the deepened mortar lines are what read as
  age. Darkened to 0.82.
- Per-cell instance colour jitter, so six meshes over 280 cells stop looking
  like six meshes. Seeded from cell position, so it is stable across builds.
- Normal strength 1.9, roughness 0.97, specular disabled. The packed ORM map
  is glossy and the roughness scalar only multiplies it, so the texture has
  to be dropped or the walls look wet.
- Torches: warm but unsaturated, `omni_attenuation` 0.55 for a gradual
  falloff, no specular. Ambient dropped to a cool near-black so the darkness
  reads blue against the fire.
- Weak torches plus low ambient gave an even grey wash with no fire in it, so
  the energy went UP (2.3) while the falloff stayed soft. The mood is bright
  pools falling into real darkness, not uniform dimness.
